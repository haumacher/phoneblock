# PhoneBlock Dongle 1.6.0

*2026-07-29*

## Added

- **The dongle now speaks your language — not only its web interface.** The
  status mail and the answer bot's greeting to a spam caller were German
  whichever language you had picked; both follow your choice now. Fourteen
  languages for the interface and the mail; the spoken announcement exists
  in eight (German, English, Spanish, French, Italian, Ukrainian, Arabic,
  Chinese) — in the others the caller hears the English recording until one
  is published, and an announcement you uploaded yourself always wins. A
  newly set-up dongle adopts your browser's language; a choice you make
  yourself is never overridden.

- **Translations come from PhoneBlock's servers, not the firmware.** Picking
  a language downloads its texts and announcement — a few seconds, shown
  next to the selector — so a new language needs no firmware update.
  English is built in for offline use. Not carrying every language also
  freed about 77 KB.

- **The status mail can go to several recipients** — up to five, separated
  by semicolons (`;`). If one address is refused, the others still receive
  it.

- **The country of your line is now a setting**, next to the language in a
  new "Language and Country" section. A newly set-up dongle takes it from
  your PhoneBlock account.

## Changed

- **A newly flashed dongle starts in English rather than German**, so a
  dongle that no browser ever configured mails and answers callers in the
  more widely understood language.

## Fixed

- **Callers on a line outside Germany are recognised again.** Every number
  without a country code was treated as German, so no caller was ever
  found. National dialling rules now follow the country you set — including
  those without a leading zero (Italy, Denmark) and North America's "1" and
  "011". Dongles already in use keep Germany until you set the country.

- **The "Protokoll" panel no longer warns about its own live view being
  closed.** 1.5.0 filtered these warnings for idle connections; what
  remained was the opposite case — the dongle still writing a reply as the
  browser left, most visibly when closing the log panel logged a warning
  into the very panel you had just closed. It still appears on the serial
  console, and genuine web-server errors are unaffected.
